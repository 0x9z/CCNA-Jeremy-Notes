# CCNA Study Notes: Chapter 2 Key Terms Explained

## 1. 10BASE-T
*   **Definition:** An Ethernet standard that supports transmission speeds up to 10 Mbps over unshielded twisted-pair (UTP) copper cabling, utilizing baseband signaling with a maximum distance of 100 meters per segment.

## 2. 100BASE-T
*   **Definition:** Also known as Fast Ethernet, this standard supports transmission speeds up to 100 Mbps over UTP copper cabling (typically Category 5 or higher).

## 3. 1000BASE-T
*   **Definition:** Also known as Gigabit Ethernet, this standard supports transmission speeds up to 1000 Mbps (1 Gbps) using all four pairs of Category 5e (or better) UTP copper cabling.

## 4. auto-MDIX
*   **Definition:** Automatic Medium Dependent Interface Crossover, a feature on modern network interfaces that automatically detects the type of connected cable (straight-through or crossover) and configures the interfaces accordingly, eliminating the need for specific cable types.

## 5. Broadcast Address
*   **Definition:** A specialized destination address (such as `255.255.255.255` at Layer 3 or `FF-FF-FF-FF-FF-FF` at Layer 2) used to send data frames or packets to all devices on the local network segment.

## 6. Cladding
*   **Definition:** The outer optical material surrounding the core of a fiber-optic cable that has a lower refractive index, trapping light inside the core through total internal reflection.

## 7. Core
*   **Definition:** The central glass or plastic strand in a fiber-optic cable through which light pulses travel to transmit data.

## 8. Crossover Cable
*   **Definition:** A type of Ethernet cable that connects devices of the same type directly (e.g., switch-to-switch or PC-to-PC) by crossing the transmit and receive signal pairs.

## 9. Electromagnetic Interference (EMI)
*   **Definition:** A disturbance caused by an external source that affects electrical circuits via electromagnetic induction or radiation, a common issue mitigated by shielding or fiber optics in networking.

## 10. Ethernet
*   **Definition:** The predominant LAN technology defined by IEEE 802.3 standards, dictating how network devices format and transmit data frames over wired physical media.

## 11. Ethernet Address
*   **Definition:** Another term for a MAC address (Media Access Control address), which is a unique 48-bit physical identifier assigned to a network interface controller.

## 12. Ethernet Frame
*   **Definition:** The Layer 2 Protocol Data Unit (PDU) for Ethernet networks, encapsulating upper-layer packets with a destination MAC, source MAC, EtherType, payload, and a Frame Check Sequence.

## 13. Ethernet Link
*   **Definition:** A communication channel established between two Ethernet-enabled devices over physical media such as twisted-pair copper or fiber optic lines.

## 14. Ethernet Port
*   **Definition:** A physical socket (such as an RJ-45 slot or fiber transceiver slot) on a networking device where an Ethernet cable is plugged in to connect to a network.

## 15. Fast Ethernet
*   **Definition:** The collective term for IEEE 802.3u Ethernet standards that operate at a speed of 100 Mbps.

## 16. Fiber-Optic Cable
*   **Definition:** A networking medium that transmits data as pulses of light through glass or plastic fibers, offering high bandwidth and immunity to electromagnetic interference over long distances.

## 17. Frame Check Sequence (FCS)
*   **Definition:** An error-detecting field (using a cyclic redundancy check) located in the trailer of an Ethernet frame, allowing the receiving device to check for corruption during transit.

## 18. Gigabit Ethernet
*   **Definition:** An extension of the Ethernet standard (IEEE 802.3z and 802.3ab) that provides a transmission rate of 1 Gbps (1000 Mbps).

## 19. IEEE
*   **Definition:** The Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers, a professional organization that develops and maintains global standards for networking technologies, including the 802 series for LANs and WLANs.

## 20. MAC Address
*   **Definition:** A globally unique 48-bit physical address burned into the ROM of a network interface card by the manufacturer, operating at Layer 2 to identify devices on a local link.

## 21. Multimode Fiber (MMF)
*   **Definition:** A type of fiber-optic cable with a larger core diameter that allows multiple light modes to propagate simultaneously, typically used for shorter-distance communications within buildings or data centers.

## 22. Network Interface Card (NIC)
*   **Definition:** A hardware component (circuit board or integrated chip) that provides a device with the ability to connect to a wired or wireless network.

## 23. RJ-45
*   **Definition:** The standard 8-position, 8-contact (8P8C) physical connector used widely for terminating twisted-pair Ethernet cables in LANs.

## 24. Single-Mode Fiber (SMF)
*   **Definition:** A type of fiber-optic cable with a very narrow core that allows only a single ray of light to pass, enabling extremely high bandwidth and long-distance transmissions across campuses or telecommunications networks.

## 25. Straight-Through Cable
*   **Definition:** A standard Ethernet cable used to connect devices of different types (such as a PC to a switch or a router to a switch), where the pinouts match on both ends.

## 26. Transceiver
*   **Definition:** A hardware device that both transmits and receives analog or digital signals, often used in networking to convert electrical signals to optical signals (e.g., SFP modules).

## 27. Unicast Address
*   **Definition:** An address used to identify a single, specific destination device on a network, ensuring data is sent only from one source to that single intended recipient.

## 28. Wired LAN
*   **Definition:** A local area network where devices are physically connected using cables (such as copper UTP or fiber optics) to share resources and communicate.

## 29. Wireless LAN (WLAN)
*   **Definition:** A local area network that utilizes radio frequency (RF) waves rather than physical cables to connect devices and facilitate communication within a limited area.
